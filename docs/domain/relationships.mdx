The master data in this ERP is not a flat list of things. It is a small graph of entities connected by a few well-chosen rule tables. This page explains that graph in the order that matches how a distributor actually thinks about the business: from the dairy at one end to the client at the other, with the distributor and the delivery group standing between them.

## The core graph

```text
Dairy ── Brand ── Product Group ─┐
              │                  │
              └── Product Type   ├── Product ── Product Link ── Distributor
              └── Packaging Type │                                    │
                                 │                                    │
                                 ▼                                    ▼
                              Tray Rule                    Group Supply Rule
                                 │                                    │
                                 ▼                                    ▼
                              Tray Type                        Delivery Group ── Client ── Billing Group
                                                                                    │
                                                                                    ▼
                                                                          Client Rate (per Product Link)
```

The rest of this page walks that graph edge by edge.

## Dairy, Brand, Product

- A **Dairy** is a physical processing facility (`master_dairy`). Each dairy has one or more brands.
- A **Brand** (`master_brand`) belongs to exactly one dairy (`dairy_id`). Every product sold in the system belongs to a brand, so every product implicitly traces back to a dairy.
- A **Product Group** (`master_product_group`) categorizes products at the top level and carries the crucial `SupplyCategory` (`MILK` or `NON_MILK`). "Cow milk 500 ml" and "curd 200 g" are separate product groups.
- A **Product Type** (`master_product_type`) is a brand-specific subclassification within a product group (for example, "toned" vs "full cream").
- A **Packaging Type** (`master_packaging_type`) captures the physical package (pouch, bottle, tetrapak).
- A **Product** (`master_product`) is the concrete SKU: a specific (brand, product\_group, product\_type, packaging\_type, packaging\_size, packaging\_unit) combination. The uniqueness constraint on that tuple prevents duplicates.

## Distributor and Product Link

The system supports multiple **Distributors** (`master_distributor`) operating simultaneously through one installation. A distributor does not sell every product from every brand — the business explicitly configures which subset each distributor handles. There are three rule tables that do this:

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Procurement Rule — what a distributor is allowed to buy from a brand">
    `distributor_procurement_rule` links `(distributor, brand, product_group)` with a `SupplyCategory`. This is the "authorized to procure" table. If a distributor has no active procurement rule for a given brand\+group\+category combination, they cannot appear as the supplier of that combination.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Product Link — a distributor's price ceiling for a specific product">
    `master_product_link` links `(distributor, product)` and is the pivot for all rates. Rates live on the link, not on the product:

    - `distributor_product_rate` — purchase and default selling rate for the distributor's link, effective-dated.
    - `master_client_rate_product` — override selling rate for a specific client on a specific link, effective-dated.

    The unique index `(distributor_id, product_id)` guarantees at most one link per (distributor, product) pair.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Group Supply Rule — which distributor supplies which delivery group and category">
    `master_group_supply_rule` links `(delivery_group, category, distributor)`. The unique index `(group_id, category)` means: for each delivery group, at most one distributor is authorized to supply MILK and at most one is authorized to supply NON\_MILK. This is where the two-distributor case shows up: Group A's milk supplier can be Distributor X and its non-milk supplier can be Distributor Y.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

The three rules combine like this: for an order line to be valid, the delivery group's supply rule for that category must point to a distributor that has an active procurement rule for the product's brand and product group, and the resolver must find a selling rate on that distributor's product link. Rate resolution is a two-tier fallback in `OrdersRepository.getSellingRate`: it first looks for the most recent active `master_client_rate_product` for `(client, product_link)` whose effective window covers the sale date; if none exists, it falls back to the most recent active `distributor_product_rate` on that link. Only if **both** are missing is a `BadRequestException` thrown.

## Delivery Group vs Billing Group

This is the single most confusing pair of concepts in the system, and getting it right is essential.

- **Delivery Group** (`master_client.delivery_group_id`) — the route the client is on. Two clients in the same delivery group are visited by the same vehicle on the same run. The order sheet is generated per delivery group.
- **Billing Group** (`master_client.billing_group_id`) — who the client is billed under. Multiple delivery groups can roll up into one billing group for invoicing, and rate cards may be shared across a billing group.

Both are foreign keys to `master_group`, but they play different roles. `master_group` has a `delivery_session` field (`NIGHT` or `MORNING`) that says when the route is normally run, and an optional `vehicle_id` that says which vehicle is the default carrier.

## Client

A **Client** (`master_client`) is a retail outlet with:

- A unique code (`code`) and human-readable name.
- A pointer to its **delivery group** and its **billing group**.
- A pointer to its **owner distributor** — the distributor who "owns" the client relationship regardless of who physically supplied the product on any given day.
- A set of `master_client_category` rows indicating which supply categories (`MILK`, `NON_MILK`, or both) the client participates in.
- A history of `master_client_rate_product` rows — one per (product\_link, effective\_from), giving the selling rate this client pays for that link at that time.

The distinction between **owner distributor** and the distributor who actually supplied a product on a given day is what makes [Distributor Transfer](/domain/paper#distributor-transfer) necessary. If Client C's owner distributor is X but Delivery Group G's milk supply rule points to Distributor Y, then Y's stock is used to serve C, and a transfer is auto-generated from Y (supplier) to X (owner) at morning submission.

## Trays

Trays are physical plastic crates. They are not products but they are tracked because they carry balances the business must reconcile.

- **Tray Type** (`master_tray_type`) — a brand-specific tray identified by color: for example, "Amul red" vs "Amul blue."
- **Product Tray Rule** (`product_tray_rule`) — the general rule that maps some subset of `(product_group, brand, product_type, packaging_type)` to a `tray_type_id`. The rule is intentionally flexible: any combination of those four fields can be `NULL` to make the rule broader. That means a given product may match multiple tray rules; the system uses the most-specific active rule to determine which tray to count against.
- **Client Tray Transaction** — the daily balance at the client (opening, taken, returned, closing).
- **Dairy Tray Transaction** — the daily balance at the dairy for each (vehicle, tray\_type).

See [Trays module](/modules/trays) for how these interact during a day.

## Vehicles and Drivers

- A **Vehicle** (`master_vehicle`) has a unique number and an optional capacity. It carries products from the dairy to clients.
- A **Driver** (`master_driver`) may be assigned to a vehicle (`vehicle_id`). Drivers do not log in; the assignment is for reporting only.
- A **Group** may reference a default vehicle (`master_group.vehicle_id`). This is a default, not a constraint — the vehicle used on any given day is determined by the [Vehicle Allocation](/modules/vehicle-allocation) records, not by the group's default.

## Users and Roles

- **Roles** are `ADMIN` and `EMPLOYEE`, stored in `roles`.
- **Users** (`users`) log in with `username`\+`password` and have exactly one role.
- Users do not own clients, distributors, or vehicles — they are operators of the system, not entities in the domain graph.

## How this graph is used in a day

At paper creation, `PaperService.generatePaperService` only walks one edge of the graph:

1. Looks up all **active delivery groups** (`master_group.is_active = true`). One `order_sheet` is auto-created per group. No client, rate, or distributor resolution happens yet.

The other edges are walked later, at order entry time, when the frontend requests the sheet grid and when the operator saves rows:

- For each sheet, the grid is populated from all clients whose `delivery_group_id` matches the sheet, filtered by their `master_client_category` rows.
- On each night/morning save, the service resolves the **owning distributor** (from `master_client.owner_distributor_id`) for billing and the **supplying distributor** (from `master_group_supply_rule` for `(delivery_group, category)`).
- It then looks up the client's active `master_client_rate_product` on the resolved supplier's `master_product_link` to determine the rate.

If any of the graph edges above is missing (no procurement rule, no supply rule, no active rate), the corresponding order line cannot be saved and the system rejects the entry. See [Business Rules — Order Entry](/domain/business-rules) for the exact validations.
